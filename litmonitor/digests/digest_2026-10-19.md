## Antioxidant culture media

### Effects of naringenin on superovulation in mice: evaluation of zygote and blastocyst development, histopathology, and molecular docking insights.

**Reprod Fertil Dev** · 2026-10-15 · PMID 42823614  
Kaya MM, Çay HA, Arslan R, et al. (8 authors)

CONTEXT: Naringenin is a citrus-derived flavanone with documented antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and steroidogenic modulatory properties; yet, its effects under gonadotropin-stimulated superovulation have not been investigated.

AIMS: This study evaluated the impact of naringenin co-administration during a pregnant mare serum gonadotropin (PMSG)/human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) superovulation protocol on zygote yield, preimplantation embryo development, and ovarian and endometrial morphology in CD-1 mice.

METHODS: Twenty-four females (8-10 weeks) received PMSG with either naringenin (20 mg/kg intraperitoneally; n = 12) or dimethyl sulfoxide vehicle (n = 12), followed by hCG 48 h later and mating with fertile males. Pronuclear-stage embryos were collected from oviducts 14-16 h post-mating and cultured in vitro for 96 h; cleavage and blastocyst formation were assessed at 24 h and 96 h respectively. Ovarian and uterine tissues were evaluated histopathologically.

KEY RESULTS: Naringenin treatment significantly increased total zygote yield. Although cleavage rate was lower in the naringenin group (45% vs 53%), blastocyst formation among cleaved embryos was markedly higher (47% vs 25%). Treated animals exhibited significantly greater corpora lutea count, corpora lutea:cell ratio, ovarian diameter, and endometrial thickness. In silico molecular docking predicted favourable binding affinities of naringenin towards the progesterone receptor and follicle-stimulating hormone receptor, generating a computational hypothesis consistent with these in vivo findings.

CONCLUSIONS: Naringenin enhances blastocyst developmental competence and post-ovulatory ovarian parameters under superovulation conditions.

IMPLICATIONS: These findings support the potential of naringenin as an adjunct to gonadotropin-based reproductive biotechnology protocols.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42823614/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1071/RD26141)

## Embryo arrest & blastulation

### Not all Day-4 embryos are equal: developmental stage predicts outcomes after single embryo transfer.

**J Assist Reprod Genet** · 2026-10-17 · PMID 42891781  
Shapira M, Haas J, Aizer A, et al. (6 authors)

PURPOSE: Evaluate outcomes of Day-4 embryo transfer according to embryonic developmental stage.

METHODS: This retrospective single-center study included fresh IVF/ICSI cycles with day-4 single embryo transfer between January 2021 and July 2025. Cycles with double embryo transfer, rescue ICSI, or missing follow-up were excluded. Transfers were divided into three groups based on embryonic stage at transfer: cleavage, morula or blastocyst-stage. Demographic and cycle characteristics, as well as clinical outcomes, were compared. Clinical pregnancy was the primary outcome.

RESULTS: A total of 669 day-4 single fresh embryo transfer cycles were included: 130 cleavage-stage (19.4%), 449 morula-stage (67.1%), and 90 blastocyst-stage (13.5%). Compared to the morula stage, the cleavage-stage group was characterized by an older age, lower oocyte yield and fewer 2PN embryos, while the blastocyst-stage group was characterized by a younger age and a similar oocyte yield and 2PN embryos. Clinical pregnancy rates increased significantly with advancing embryonic stage (10.0%, 32.96%, and 48.89%, respectively; p < 0.05). In multivariable analysis, only increasing maternal age (OR 0.92, 95% CI 0.88-0.96) and cleavage-stage transfer (OR 0.37, 95% CI 0.18-0.78) remained negatively associated with clinical pregnancy.

CONCLUSIONS: Fresh day 4 embryo transfer outcomes are strongly influenced by embryonic developmental stage. Cleavage-stage embryos on day 4 are associated with poor pregnancy rates and may benefit from extended culture. In contrast, day 4 morula or blastocyst transfer represents a viable clinical option, particularly when logistical constraints preclude day 5 transfer.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42891781/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10815-026-04001-1)

### Association Between Sperm DNA Fragmentation Index and Outcomes of Fresh Embryo Transfer in IVF/ICSI Cycles: A Retrospective Cohort Study.

**Andrology** · 2026-10-16 · PMID 42885487  
Yang R, Sun M, Chen J, et al. (7 authors)

_Also matches_: Sperm DNA fragmentation (single-strand) & ART

OBJECTIVE: This study explored the impact of the sperm DNA fragmentation index (DFI) on clinical outcomes and infant safety in in vitro fertilization/intracytoplasmic sperm injection (IVF/ICSI) among infertile couples.

METHODS: A retrospective cohort study analyzed clinical data from 4520 infertile couples undergoing fresh embryo transfer in IVF/ICSI cycles at Fujian Maternity and Child Health Hospital between June 2018 and January 2025. Participants were stratified according to BMI categories. The primary outcomes were semen parameters, while secondary outcomes included embryo-related, pregnancy, and fetal outcomes. Associations between sperm DFI and reproductive outcomes were evaluated using multivariable linear and logistic regression models. Restricted cubic spline models were applied to explore potential nonlinear relationships. Subgroup analyses were performed according to fertilization method (IVF or ICSI), and sensitivity analyses were conducted by modeling DFI as a continuous variable and by applying an alternative DFI cutoff value of 20%.

RESULTS: Elevated sperm DFI was significantly associated with impaired semen quality across BMI strata. Compared with men with DFI <15%, those with DFI ≥15% exhibited lower sperm concentration, total sperm count, sperm motility, and normal morphology rate, with the strongest associations observed among men with high BMI. Regarding embryo-related outcomes, elevated DFI was associated with a lower fertilization rate among men with high BMI, whereas in the low-BMI subgroup, elevated DFI was associated with a higher high-quality embryo rate (Adjusted β = 10.24, 95% CI: 0.78 to 19.70). In the primary analyses, no significant associations were observed between sperm DFI and blastocyst formation, clinical pregnancy, abortion, live birth, fetal aneuploidy, or birth defects. Sensitivity analyses using DFI as a continuous variable and an alternative cutoff value of 20% yielded results generally consistent with the primary analyses. Subgroup analyses stratified by fertilization method demonstrated comparable associations between sperm DFI and reproductive outcomes, supporting the robustness of the findings.

CONCLUSION: Sperm DFI is significantly associated with the quality of semen and embryo development in assisted reproduction. A positive association with high-quality embryo rate was observed in the low-BMI subgroup; however, this finding should be interpreted cautiously because of the relatively small sample size in the low BMI subgroup.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42885487/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/andr.70379)

### Cumulus cell DNA fragmentation as non-invasive biomarker of equine oocyte quality in in vitro embryo production.

**Reprod Fertil Dev** · 2026-10-15 · PMID 42833617  
Zhang L, Smith JM, Zhang R, et al. (7 authors)

_Also matches_: Sperm DNA fragmentation (single-strand) & ART

CONTEXT: The efficiency of in vitro embryo production in animal species is constrained by the absence of practical, non-invasive biomarkers to assess oocyte quality. Cumulus cell DNA fragmentation index (CC-DFI) has been proposed as a biomarker in humans, but data in animals remain limited.

AIMS: This study investigated the association of CC-DFI with in vitro maturation (IVM) and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) outcomes in horses, and its relationship with mare-related factors, autumnal transition period, cumulus cell viability, and cumulus expansion.

METHODS: Cumulus-oocyte complexes (COCs) retrieved via ovum pick-up were subjected to IVM. Immediately after denudation, CC-DFI for each mare was assessed using a chromatin dispersion assay across 54 ICSI sessions. Viability was assessed based on membrane integrity, and expansion was quantified as the relative change in total COC area. Only mature oocytes were selected for ICSI and subsequent in vitro culture.

KEY RESULTS: Oocyte maturation rate and cleavage rate were not associated with CC-DFI, whereas total blastocyst rate and yield were significantly negatively associated with CC-DFI. Blastocyst yield at day 7 was also significantly negatively associated with CC-DFI, whereas no associations were found with grade 1 blastocysts. No significant associations of CC-DFI with mare-related factors, autumnal transition period, cumulus cell viability, and cumulus expansion were observed.

CONCLUSIONS: In conclusion, this study supports CC-DFI as a potential biomarker for predicting oocyte developmental potential in equine ICSI, particularly blastocyst formation.

IMPLICATIONS: CC-DFI could help improve oocyte selection and embryo production efficiency in equine ICSI programs, but further validation is required before routine clinical use.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42833617/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1071/RD26148)

## MTHFR / methylation & ART

### Could glyoxal be an alternative fixative to paraformaldehyde in human spermatozoa?

**J Assist Reprod Genet** · 2026-10-14 · PMID 42873433  
Celikkan FT, Erkan E, Cinar FU, et al. (8 authors)

PURPOSE: Our previous studies demonstrated the superiority of paraformaldehyde (PFA) over glyoxal (Gly) fixation in stem cells, oocytes, and embryos. Here, we compared these two aldehyde-based fixatives in human spermatozoa to determine whether Gly, which is reported to better preserve immunoreactivity, can provide effective and consistent fixation comparable to PFA.

METHODS: The fixation performance of PFA (3.5%) and Gly (3%), with or without 0.01% Triton-X (TX) permeabilization, was evaluated side-by-side in live and fixed human spermatozoa. Analyses included total protein quantification by Western blotting, epitope detection by super-resolution confocal laser scanning microscopy, ultrastructural assessment by scanning electron microscopy, and DNA fragmentation analysis by TUNEL assay coupled with flow cytometry.

RESULTS: Protein fixation assays demonstrated that PFA was superior to Gly in preserving total cellular proteins. Similarly, the sperm-specific membrane protein IZUMO1 was better retained after PFA fixation. In contrast, Gly combined with subsequent TX permeabilization provided superior labeling of nuclear DNA, the acrosomal protein ACRBP, a nuclear DNA-methylating enzyme DNMT3A and TUNEL-based detection of DNA fragmentation compared with all PFA-based fixation conditions. No significant differences between fixatives were observed in ultrastructural preservation, post-fixation live mitochondrial staining.

CONCLUSION: PFA was more effective for preserving total and membrane-associated proteins in human spermatozoa, whereas Gly represented a promising and less toxic alternative for studies targeting nuclear components and DNA-associated molecules. These findings indicate that the choice of fixative should be determined according to the specific cellular targets under investigation, particularly cytoplasmic, membrane-associated, or nuclear proteins and enzymes.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42873433/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10815-026-04060-4)

## PGT-A in advanced maternal age

### Should PGT-A be routinely utilized for advanced maternal age?

**J Assist Reprod Genet** · 2026-10-13 · PMID 42867733  
Chin AHB, Alon I, Sun N

Preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy (PGT-A) is often presented to women of advanced maternal age as a logical safeguard against miscarriage and chromosomal abnormality. Yet intuitive rationale and biological plausibility do not establish net clinical benefit. PGT-A does not improve oocyte or embryo quality; it ranks embryos that already exist. The appropriate objective endpoint is therefore cumulative live birth from an initiated treatment strategy, not implantation after transfer of a selected euploid embryo. Randomized clinical trials, contemporary systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and professional guidance have not shown that routine PGT-A improves cumulative live birth rates in older women, while mosaicism, limitations of trophectoderm sampling, invasive biopsy, variability in laboratory protocols, freeze-thaw requirements, embryo exclusion, financial cost, and treatment delay can impose material harms. These concerns are amplified in older women with fewer transferable blastocysts. Sociocultural anxiety about disability and delayed childbearing, reinforced by commercial promotion, clinical normalization, and misleading risk communication, can further compromise autonomous choice. PGT-A may be utilized selectively for older women, especially when several blastocysts are available and patients prioritize avoiding an aneuploid transfer, but advanced maternal age alone should not convert an uncertain add-on into a routine clinical strategy.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42867733/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10815-026-04049-z)

## Recurrent implantation failure

### Possible role of bacterial infection in embryo implantation failure.

**Reprod Fertil Dev** · 2026-10-15 · PMID 42805688  
Moghoofei M, Shahbazi R, Rezaei M, et al. (9 authors)

Embryo implantation is a critical and complex process that requires coordination between the embryo and the receptive endometrium for its success. However, a multitude of factors have the potential to contribute to implantation failure. Among these factors, bacterial infections in the uterine environment are of particular concern and are increasingly being considered as a contributing factor to implantation failure. The presence of pathogenic bacteria has been demonstrated to stimulate inflammatory responses, alter the endometrial microbiome, interfere with critical signaling pathways, and finally change the uterus microenvironments that are required for implantation. We aimed to review bacterial infection as an important potential etiology of chronic endometritis pathophysiology and its correlation with implantation failure.

### Assisted reproductive technologies on-chip: the application of microfluidics and optics to sperm assessment and sorting.

**Reprod Fertil Dev** · 2026-10-15 · PMID 42843648  
Seymour KR, Guo Y, Cannarozzo M, et al. (6 authors)

_Also matches_: ZyMot / microfluidic sperm selection

CONTEXT: Semen evaluation plays a key role in the success of assisted reproductive technologies (ART) as it allows high-quality spermatozoa to be selected. Conventional analysis methods are widely performed despite drawbacks, including subjectivity, potential sample damage, high costs and the need for specialised personnel, highlighting the demand for standardised, accessible and minimally invasive approaches.

AIMS: This review describes the potential of microfluidics and optical trapping (OT) as alternative approaches for semen assessment and selection, exploring their possible combination and integration into ART workflows.

METHODS: A literature review was conducted to describe in vitro assessment of spermatozoa and explore conventional methods for selection, separation and sorting. Microfluidics and OT were introduced from basic concepts to applications in biology. Their application to assisted reproduction was then examined, focusing on sperm sorting, assessment and IVF and intracytoplasmic sperm injection techniques.

KEY RESULTS: Microfluidic systems, exploiting laminar flow within microchannels, represent a non-invasive approach to sperm selection. They enable motile spermatozoa to be separated from immotile or abnormal spermatozoa while reducing centrifugation-related phenomena such as mechanical stress and DNA fragmentation. OT allows precise manipulation of spermatozoa and quantitative investigation of sperm motility, swimming force and cellular behaviour. The integrated use of Raman spectroscopy and fluorescence imaging allows cellular activity assessment.

CONCLUSIONS: Microfluidics and OT provide complementary approaches for sperm assessment and sorting, integrating non-invasive selection strategies with single-cell manipulation and analysis.

IMPLICATIONS: The integration of these approaches into a single optofluidic device may support incorporation into ART workflows, promoting improved semen selection and analysis.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42843648/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1071/RD26043)

## Time-lapse / morphokinetic embryo selection

### Spatiotemporal deep learning with visualization predicts fertilization for early rescue intracytoplasmic sperm injection.

**F S Sci** · 2026-10-16 · PMID 42887774  
Saito M, Haraguchi H, Yamamoto N, et al. (11 authors)

OBJECTIVE: To determine whether a deep learning (DL) model trained on time-lapse videos predicts fertilization following short-term insemination more accurately than senior embryologists, particularly in morphologically difficult cases, to visualize classification regions, and to evaluate whether a shortened observation window could still support accurate classification before early rescue intracytoplasmic sperm injection (r-ICSI).

DESIGN: Single-center retrospective study.

SUBJECTS: A total of 1,331 embryos from 250 short-term insemination cycles were identified. After excluding immature and degenerated oocytes, 1,016 embryos (770 fertilized, 246 unfertilized) were analyzed and split at the patient level into training and validation (n = 925) and test (n = 91) sets.

EXPOSURE: Time-lapse videos recorded from 4.5 to 8 h post-insemination were input into a spatiotemporal convolutional neural network trained with 5-fold cross-validation and compared with independent classification by 5 senior embryologists (>5 years' experience). Holdout embryos were stratified by embryologist accuracy, and classification regions were visualized. Model performance was also assessed using progressively truncated videos.

MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES: Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC-AUC), accuracy, recall, specificity, precision, negative predictive value (NPV), and F1 score on the holdout test set, and stratum-specific accuracy.

RESULTS: The DL model achieved a mean ROC-AUC of 0.938 (ensemble model, 0.968) and significantly higher accuracy (0.884 versus 0.802), recall (0.946 versus 0.837), NPV (0.802 versus 0.557), and F1 score (0.927 versus 0.867) than senior embryologists. In moderate- and low-accuracy cases (≤3 of 5 embryologists correct), the DL model classified fertilized embryos significantly more accurately than embryologists (P < 0.05). Even when trained on videos truncated at 6 h, recall and NPV remained significantly higher than embryologists using the full 4.5-8.0 h videos. Visualization showed cytoplasmic highlighting in fertilized embryos and polar body and perivitelline space highlighting in unfertilized embryos.

CONCLUSION: A DL model with region visualization achieved significantly higher predictive accuracy than senior embryologists, including for fertilized embryos in cases prone to human misclassification, and retained higher recall and NPV even when trained on videos truncated at 6 h. This suggests potential utility for early r-ICSI, although multicenter prospective validation is required.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42887774/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xfss.2026.10.004)
