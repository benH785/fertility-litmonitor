## Endometrial receptivity testing (ERA / ER-Map)

### Endometrial compaction and ongoing pregnancy in modified natural cycle frozen euploid embryo transfer: a prospective multicentre cohort study with standardized transvaginal ultrasound assessment.

**Hum Reprod** · 2026-10-25 · PMID 42926142  
De la Torre Pérez E, Castillo-Farfán JC, Carratalá-Munuera MC, et al. (8 authors)

STUDY QUESTION: Does endometrial compaction, assessed by transvaginal ultrasound at two measurement time points, predict ongoing pregnancy in patients undergoing single euploid blastocyst transfer in a modified natural cycle frozen embryo transfer (mNC-FET)?

SUMMARY ANSWER: Endometrial compaction of ≥5% between the day of ovulation triggering and the day of embryo transfer was not associated with ongoing pregnancy rate or any secondary reproductive outcome in this prospective multicentre cohort.

WHAT IS KNOWN ALREADY: Endometrial compaction-a reduction in endometrial thickness (EMT) following progesterone-induced secretory transformation-has been proposed as a potential marker of endometrial receptivity in FET cycles. However, existing evidence is conflicting. Retrospective studies have reported positive associations between compaction and ongoing pregnancy in programmed cycles, while prospective data, including the only prior study restricted to euploid single FET, have not confirmed this relationship. Crucially, most studies have used transabdominal ultrasound for the transfer-day measurement, a recognized source of measurement error. No prospective study has examined compaction exclusively in mNC-FET using transvaginal ultrasound at both time points.

STUDY DESIGN, SIZE, DURATION: Prospective multicentre observational cohort study conducted between March 2023 and March 2025 across six assisted reproduction units of Instituto Bernabeu group (Spain). A total of 207 patients who met all inclusion criteria and completed the full protocol were analysed.

PARTICIPANTS/MATERIALS, SETTING, METHODS: Women aged 18-50 years undergoing mNC-FET with a single euploid blastocyst (confirmed by preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy via next-generation sequencing) were included. EMT was measured transvaginally at T1 (day of hCG trigger) and T2 (day of embryo transfer). Compaction was defined as a ≥5% reduction in EMT from T1 to T2. Additional thresholds of ≥10% and ≥15% were evaluated as sensitivity analyses. The primary outcome was ongoing pregnancy, defined as foetal cardiac activity at 10 weeks of gestation. Multivariable logistic regression was performed, adjusting for age, previous live births, previous miscarriages, BMI, and oocyte origin.

MAIN RESULTS AND THE ROLE OF CHANCE: Of 207 patients, 58 (28.0%) met the compaction criterion (≥5%). The compaction group had a significantly greater T1 EMT (9.55 ± 1.54 mm vs 8.39 ± 1.14 mm, P < 0.001) and a lower T2 EMT (7.91 ± 1.19 mm vs 9.75 ± 1.55 mm, P < 0.001). Ongoing pregnancy rates were 50.0% in the compaction group versus 53.7% in the non-compaction group (P = 0.633). This difference was not significant on multivariable analysis (adjusted odds ratio 0.80, 95% CI 0.38-1.67, P = 0.546). The null association was consistent across all compaction thresholds (≥10%: P = 0.674; ≥15%: P = 0.928) and across all secondary outcomes, including clinical pregnancy rate (56.9% vs 59.1%, P = 0.777), biochemical pregnancy loss (10.8% vs 8.3%, P = 0.737), and early pregnancy loss rate (12.1% vs 9.1%, P = 0.734). The overall ongoing pregnancy rate was 52.7% (109/207).

LIMITATIONS, REASONS FOR CAUTION: The distribution between groups was asymmetric (28% compaction vs 72% non-compaction), deviating from the balanced allocation anticipated at protocol design, which may reduce statistical power. Although both measurements were transvaginal, a subset of T1 measurements from international patients were performed at external centres and validated remotely by Instituto Bernabeu's physicians. Live birth data were only partially available at the time of analysis; however, ongoing pregnancy at 10 weeks is a validated and widely accepted surrogate for live birth in reproductive medicine trials.

WIDER IMPLICATIONS OF THE FINDINGS: These findings do not support the hypothesis that endometrial compaction is associated with improved reproductive outcomes in mNC-FET with euploid embryos. The consistent null result across multiple thresholds and outcomes, obtained with a fully standardized transvaginal ultrasound methodology, strengthens the current evidence base. Routine assessment of endometrial compaction with the aim of identifying patients with a favourable prognosis does not appear to be clinically justified in this setting; indeed, acting upon compaction status could lead to unnecessary cycle cancellations in patients who would otherwise achieve ongoing pregnancy.

The funding body had no role in the study design, data collection, analysis or interpretation of the data, preparation of the manuscript, or decision to submit the manuscript for publication.

DISCLOSURES: The authors declare no competing interests related to the present study. No personal payments, financial incentives, or commercial benefits were received by any author in relation to this work.

### The Gut-Immune-Endometrial Axis in Reproductive Failure: a Structured Narrative Review of Intestinal Dysbiosis, Systemic Inflammation, and Chronic Endometritis.

**Reprod Sci** · 2026-10-22 · PMID 42918965  
Rodrigues RS, Fontenele PA, Rocha JC, et al. (4 authors)

Chronic endometritis (CE) is a persistent inflammatory condition of the endometrium characterized by stromal plasma cell infiltration and is increasingly recognized in women with infertility, recurrent implantation failure (RIF), and recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL). Although traditionally considered a localized infectious process, heterogeneous treatment responses and recurrence suggest that broader immunological mechanisms may contribute in selected patients. This structured narrative review synthesizes clinical and mechanistic evidence relevant to a possible gut-immune-endometrial axis. A structured PubMed/MEDLINE search was conducted from database inception to December 31, 2025, and reference lists of relevant reviews and key primary studies were screened. Evidence was synthesized thematically across clinical, immunological, and translational domains. CE is associated with infertility and RPL, whereas findings in RIF populations are heterogeneous and strongly dependent on diagnostic criteria. CE is characterized by plasma cell infiltration, pro-inflammatory signaling, and activation of pathways including toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)-nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB). Separately, intestinal dysbiosis and barrier dysfunction may permit systemic translocation of microbial products such as lipopolysaccharide, thereby promoting low-grade inflammatory signaling. However, no direct human study has yet linked gut dysbiosis or endotoxemia to histologically confirmed CE within the same cohort. The proposed axis should therefore be regarded as a hypothesis-generating framework rather than an established causal pathway. CE is biologically heterogeneous and may arise from infectious, local microbial, immune-mediated, or systemic-inflammatory mechanisms that can overlap. Reverse causality and shared confounding factors are also plausible. At present, the model does not justify routine microbiome testing or gut-directed treatment for CE, but it defines priorities for prospective studies integrating microbiome profiling, systemic immune markers, and standardized endometrial phenotyping.

### From organoids to assembloids: modeling tissue crosstalk in female reproductive biology and disease.

**Mol Hum Reprod** · 2026-10-19 · PMID 42895300  
Zhang Q, Yu S, Wang Q, et al. (4 authors)

The female reproductive tract is a multi-tissue system in which epithelial, stromal, immune, and vascular cells coordinate responses to hormonal signals. Single-tissue organoids have advanced epithelial modeling in vitro but cannot capture the inter-tissue communication underlying key reproductive processes. Assembloids, which recombine cells from distinct tissue compartments in three-dimensional space, offer a means to restore these missing interactions. This review examines reproductive assembloid research across three biology-driven contexts. In menstrual cycle modeling, organoid-based systems have recapitulated epithelial breakdown and regeneration, yet stromal, immune, and vascular integration remain incomplete. In implantation modeling, embryo-endometrial co-cultures and receptive assembloids have begun to capture maternal-fetal crosstalk, though immune and vascular dimensions are largely missing. In endometriosis and adenomyosis modeling, assembloids have started to reconstruct pathogenic microenvironments that single organoids cannot replicate. Three issues are examined in depth. The first is the gap between what current models can and cannot reveal about reproductive biology. The second is the risk that added complexity may only confirm findings already established in vivo. The third is the point at which an additional tissue component stops contributing new information. Progress in this field is likely to depend not on greater structural complexity but on questions that only multi-tissue models can address.

## Sperm DNA fragmentation (single-strand) & ART

### Different Proportions of Round-Headed Spermatozoa: Impacts on Sperm Function and IVF/ICSI Outcomes.

**Andrology** · 2026-10-21 · PMID 42908247  
Long S, Yang F, Yu L, et al. (8 authors)

BACKGROUND: Although complete globozoospermia is well characterized, it is clinically rare. Partial globozoospermia is more prevalent, yet the impact of varying round-headed sperm ratios on disease severity remains unclear. Notably, even low proportions often signal compromised semen quality and spermatogenic dysfunction. This study investigates the impact of these proportions on sperm function and ART outcomes to provide evidence for individualized clinical management.

METHODS: This was a retrospective case-control study. After balancing baseline characteristics by propensity score matching (PSM), 728 subjects were included in both the round-headed sperm group (proportion ≥ 5%) and the control group. A machine learning-based automatic sperm morphology analysis system was used to quantify the proportion of round-headed sperm and their head parameters. Sperm function indicators (DNA integrity, acrosome function, mitochondrial membrane potential) were detected, and 166 couples from the round-headed sperm group who received IVF/ICSI were included for reproductive outcome analysis.

RESULTS: After PSM, the round-headed sperm group showed significantly lower normal morphology rate, sperm kinematic parameters, nucleoprotein maturation, acrosome integrity, and acrosin activity compared with the control group. The sperm DNA fragmentation index (DFI) and high DNA stainability (HDS) were higher in the round-headed sperm group (p < 0.001). ROC curve analysis based on clinical pregnancy outcomes determined that the cut-off value for the proportion of round-headed sperm is 7.2%. The high-proportion group (> 7.2%) exhibited significantly lower IVF fertilization rate and high-quality embryo rate than the low-proportion group (≤ 7.2%) (p < 0.01). In ICSI cycles, the high-proportion group had significantly lower high-quality embryo rate (48.48% vs. 40.33%, p = 0.016), clinical pregnancy rate (58.82% vs. 41.43%, p = 0.048), and live birth rate (50.98% vs. 32.86%, p = 0.028) than the low-proportion group.

CONCLUSIONS: The proportion of round-headed sperm can serve as an objective predictor for sperm function and outcomes before assisted reproductive technology treatment.

## ZyMot / microfluidic sperm selection

### Pregnancy Outcomes of Intrauterine Insemination Using Microfluidics ZyMot Device for Patients With High Sperm DNA Fragmentation: An Exploratory Retrospective Study.

**Andrology** · 2026-10-24 · PMID 42920326  
Kesidou O, Hamze H, Hemmings R, et al. (4 authors)

BACKGROUND: High sperm DNA fragmentation affects male fertility and negatively correlates with sperm survival and pregnancy rates. The use of microfluidic devices allows more rigorous sperm selection, avoiding the centrifugation which is detrimental to DNA integrity.

OBJECTIVES: To determine whether the microfluidic ZyMot sperm selection method increases the intrauterine insemination (IUI) pregnancy rate in case of high sperm DNA fragmentation.

MATERIALS/METHODS: Retrospective cohort exploratory study including 132 patients with high sperm DNA fragmentation in the time period between December 2022 and December 2024. The microfluidic ZyMot device and density gradient were used to prepare sperm for insemination in 50 and 82 patients, respectively. The study included patients undergoing IUI cycles with fresh sperm samples with high DNA fragmentation, equal or more than 20%, tested within 6 months of the insemination.

RESULTS: The IUI pregnancy rates (PR) with the ZyMot method were 8% and for density gradient 9.8%, with no significant difference, p = 1.00. The total motile sperm count (TSM) was significantly higher in the gradient group (35.0 vs. 12.3, p-value < 0.001). For all patients combined with sperm DNA fragmentation index (DFI) between 20% and 30%, the PR was 12.3% and with DFI more than 30% the PR was 3.9%.

DISCUSSION: This exploratory research shows that the use of the microfluidic ZyMot device for sperm preparation in IUI for patients with high DNA fragmentation was not associated with a significant increase in pregnancy rate; pregnancy outcomes are comparable between two preparation methods: ZyMot and density gradient.

CONCLUSION: While the microfluidic ZyMot method allows more refined sperm selection with lower DFI, its pregnancy outcomes are similar to those of density gradient wash in IUI cycles. Both methods can be used for sperm preparation. The observed difference in pregnancy rates between patients with a DFI of 20%-30% and those with a DFI >30% may indicate a possible trend toward lower IUI success at higher DFI levels, although further studies are needed to confirm this association.

LIMITATIONS, REASONS FOR CAUTION: The retrospective study has a high risk of type II error due to small number of participants. Given the exploratory nature, repeated cycles from the same patient may have been included and statistical comparisons should therefore be interpreted with caution.
