## DuoStim / luteal-phase stimulation

### Oocyte yield according to initiation timing in random-start controlled ovarian hyperstimulation.

**J Assist Reprod Genet** · 2026-11-04 · PMID 42980958  
Yoon SY, Choi J, Kim SE, et al. (5 authors)

PURPOSE: Random-start controlled ovarian hyperstimulation (COH) is an established strategy for fertility preservation (FP) in cancer patients. Although previous studies have demonstrated its efficacy using a binary classification of menstrual cycle phases (follicular vs. luteal), this approach might not fully reflect dynamic endocrine changes throughout the cycle. This study used a subdivided phase classification to evaluate the ovarian response according to the menstrual cycle phase at random-start COH initiation and further assessed the outcomes according to the ovarian reserve.

METHODS: This retrospective study included 255 cancer patients who underwent random-start COH. Patients were categorized into five menstrual cycle phases at the time of stimulation initiation: early follicular (EF), late follicular (LF), early luteal (EL), mid-luteal (ML), and late luteal (LL). Oocyte yield, total gonadotropin dose, and stimulation duration were compared among groups. A subgroup analysis was conducted in patients with low ovarian reserve (anti-Müllerian hormone [AMH] < 1.2 ng/mL).

RESULTS: Total oocyte yield was significantly higher in the LL group than in the EF group, though with a greater total gonadotropin dose and longer stimulation duration. Among patients with low ovarian reserve, luteal phase initiation was associated with significantly higher total and mature metaphase II (MII) oocyte counts compared with follicular phase initiation.

CONCLUSIONS: The menstrual cycle phase at stimulation initiation influences ovarian response during random-start COH. The LL phase represents an effective window for stimulation initiation, and luteal phase initiation is particularly advantageous for maximizing oocyte yield in patients with low ovarian reserve who require urgent FP.

## Embryo arrest & blastulation

### Morphokinetic characterization of human embryos derived from capacitation in vitro maturation versus controlled ovarian stimulation with intracytoplasmic sperm injection: a retrospective cohort study using time-lapse imaging.

**J Assist Reprod Genet** · 2026-11-05 · PMID 42986520  
Dang AH, Nguyen TC, Pham TD, et al. (10 authors)

_Also matches_: Time-lapse / morphokinetic embryo selection

PURPOSE: To characterize the morphokinetic profile of blastocyst-stage embryos derived from biphasic capacitation in vitro maturation (CAPA-IVM), using a contemporaneous cohort of controlled ovarian stimulation with intracytoplasmic sperm injection (COS-ICSI) embryos as the reference, with standardized time-lapse imaging.

METHODS: This retrospective cohort study was conducted at an assisted reproduction center from March 2021 to December 2024. Eligible women were aged < 40 years and had no prior fertilization failure, gamete donation, planned preimplantation genetic testing, or significant male factor infertility. CAPA-IVM and COS-ICSI cycles were matched 1:1 on a propensity score built from female age and the number of analyzable embryos in the cycle. Embryos were cultured to the blastocyst stage with time-lapse monitoring. Morphokinetic timings were compared using crude, adjusted mixed-effects, and trajectory analyses, with a crude subgroup analysis of good-quality G1/G2 embryos.

RESULTS: Overall, 232 couples contributed 876 embryos (421 CAPA-IVM and 455 COS-ICSI). In crude analyses, CAPA-IVM versus COS-ICSI embryos showed later pronuclear appearance (+ 0.71 h; p < 0.001), earlier pronuclear fading (- 0.92 h; p < 0.001), earlier t3 (- 1.42 h; p < 0.001), and earlier t5 (- 1.40 h; p = 0.017), but later compaction (+ 2.00 h; p < 0.01) and blastulation (+ 1.67 h; p < 0.01). Later pronuclear appearance, earlier pronuclear fading, earlier t3, and earlier t5 with CAPA-IVM versus COS-ICSI remained statistically significant after adjustment. There was a lower proportion of good-quality G1/G2 embryos after CAPA-IVM versus COS-ICSI (29% vs. 39%; p < 0.01). In the G1/G2 embryo subset, CAPA-IVM showed later pronuclear appearance but earlier pronuclear fading, t3, and t4.

CONCLUSIONS: CAPA-IVM embryos showed a distinct, non-uniform morphokinetic profile, highlighting the need for maturation pathway-specific interpretation of time-lapse data.

### Nicotinamide mononucleotide supports the development of bovine embryo through enhancement of mitochondrial function in morula.

**Reproduction** · 2026-11-04 · PMID 42865078  
Ye S, Hashimoto S, Yamanaka M, et al. (8 authors)

In brief: We describe how NAD+ precursors in embryo culture media promote embryonic development into blastocysts by enhancing NAD+ level in embryos. Abstract: Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) is an important regulator of cellular physiological processes. However, in the culture environment, NAD+ levels are predicted to decrease below physiological requirements. Therefore, we examined the effects of the NAD+ precursor nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) on NAD+ levels and viability of mammalian fertilized ova. NAD+ levels decreased during in-vitro culture, but this decrease was alleviated by NMN. Nicotinamide mononucleotide also increased the blastocyst formation rate and accelerated the developmental rate. Transcriptome analysis showed that NMN induced significant changes in the expression of genes involved in mitochondrial function in morula, and that NMN increased mitochondrial membrane potential, oxygen consumption rate and mitochondrial DNA copy number of morula while reducing reactive oxygen species levels. The addition of NMN to the embryo culture medium increased NAD+ levels in embryos, boosted mitochondrial function at the morula stage, and enhanced their ability to develop into blastocysts.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42865078/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/reprod/xaag128)

### LncRNA Jpx recruits EEF1A1 to enhance Cul4b mRNA stability and translation efficiency, safeguarding mitophagy and spermiogenesis in mice.

**Reproduction** · 2026-11-03 · PMID 42979839  
Guo X, Wang R, Chen X, et al. (6 authors)

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are abundantly expressed in the testis, but their roles in male fertility remain largely uncharacterized. Here, we generated Jpx knockout (KO) mice and found that Jpx deficiency did not affect spermatogonial proliferation or meiosis, but predominantly impaired the post-meiotic phase of spermiogenesis. KO males exhibited reduced testis size, prolonged reproductive cycles, smaller litters, decreased sperm count, and impaired progressive motility. Their spermatozoa also showed compromised in vitro fertilization (IVF) efficiency and lower blastocyst formation rates. Ultrastructurally, KO spermatozoa had elevated teratozoospermia manifested as excessive residual cytoplasm with dispersed mitochondria and autophagic vacuoles, as well as severe midpiece mitochondrial disorganization, including disrupted sheath assembly, cristae loss, and vacuolization. Metabolomics revealed a profound defect in spermatozoal mitochondrial energy metabolism, with significant reductions in multiple glycolytic and TCA cycle intermediates (e.g., G6P and isocitrate) as well as key energy molecules (ATP, GTP, and NAD+). Mechanistically, the predominantly cytoplasmic Jpx directly bound the elongation factor EEF1A1 to form a ribonucleoprotein complex, which specifically attached to the 3' untranslated region (3'UTR) of Cul4b mRNA, thereby stabilizing the transcript and enhancing its translation. Consequently, Jpx ablation led to a substantial reduction in Cul4b, defective mitophagy (as evidenced by decreased PINK1 and LC3-II/I ratio and increased p62), mitochondrial dysfunction, and elevated oxidative stress in spermatogenic cells. Together, our findings identify Jpx as an essential post-transcriptional regulator that control mitophagy via the EEF1A1-dependent stabilization and translational promotion of Cul4b, thereby ensuring mitochondrial homeostasis, metabolic competence, and functional spermatozoon maturation.

## Endometrial receptivity testing (ERA / ER-Map)

### Circular RNA-NLRP3 Crosstalk in Reproductive Biology: A Critical Appraisal of Direct Evidence and Testable Hypotheses.

**Reprod Sci** · 2026-11-03 · PMID 42977869  
Hsu CY, Abed HS, Ahmad I, et al. (10 authors)

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are abundant and developmentally regulated in gonads, gametes, and preimplantation embryos, whereas NLRP3 inflammasome signaling has context-dependent roles in ovarian aging, spermatogenic stress, endometrial receptivity, implantation, and placental disease. Whether these two evidence streams constitute a functional regulatory axis in mammalian gametogenesis or early embryogenesis remains uncertain. In this regard, this review article aimed to identify direct reproductive mechanisms; reproductive functions without a demonstrated circRNA-NLRP3 bridge, association, or bioinformatic prediction; and mechanistic extrapolations from non-reproductive systems. Direct reproductive evidence was limited to four placental/trophoblast or granulosa-cell examples: circ_0001438/miR-942/NLRP3 signaling in preeclampsia-associated trophoblast models; circ_0081343-RBM8A regulation of NLRP3-linked pyroptosis in fetal-growth-restriction models; circ_0070987-associated pyroptosis in a dihydrotestosterone-treated granulosa-cell line; and hsa_circ_0003218 overexpression, which reduced NLRP3-pathway readouts in high-glucose HTR-8/SVneo trophoblasts in a gestational-diabetes study. No direct functional study was identified in mammalian sperm, oocytes, zygotes, cleavage-stage embryos, blastocysts, or the male germ line. Accordingly, circRNA detection, co-expression, host-gene enrichment, and predicted competing endogenous RNA networks should be regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than as proof of NLRP3 regulation. CircRNAs in seminal plasma and maternal blood show candidate biomarker potential, but external validation remains limited, and no combined reproductive circRNA-NLRP3 panel has been validated. Similarly, circRNA mimics, antisense approaches, circRNA-encoded peptides, extracellular vesicle (EVs) delivery, and combined circRNA/NLRP3 therapy remain preclinical or hypothetical in terms of reproduction. The most immediate priorities are cell-type- and stage-resolved perturbation studies, binding and rescue experiments, quantitative inflammasome readouts, reproductive safety assessment, and prospective biomarker validation.

[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42977869/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43032-026-02239-9)

## MTHFR / methylation & ART

### From folate to the epigenetic clock: one-carbon metabolism in oocyte quality and ovarian ageing.

**Hum Reprod Update** · 2026-11-06 · PMID 42992622  
Fu X, Yang Y, Jiang L, et al. (12 authors)

_Also matches_: Methylfolate vs folic acid in IVF

BACKGROUND: One-carbon metabolism (OCM) supports nucleotide synthesis and S-adenosylmethionine (SAM)-dependent methylation; its links to sulfur metabolism connect it to glutathione (GSH) synthesis and hydrogen sulfide (H2S) signalling. These functions are relevant to oocyte growth, epigenetic programming, and developmental competence, but their contribution to ovarian ageing remains incompletely understood. Existing reviews address pregnancy, embryo development, or female fertility broadly, leaving OCM in stage-specific oocyte biology and ageing insufficiently integrated.

OBJECTIVE AND RATIONALE: This review examines OCM in oocyte development, epigenetic regulation, and ovarian ageing, focusing on substrate supply, enzyme regulation, and methyl-donor metabolism. It evaluates experimental and clinical evidence on metabolic disturbances and nutritional interventions, and identifies knowledge gaps that limit clinical translation.

SEARCH METHODS: PubMed/MEDLINE was searched for English-language literature published from January 2008 to September 2026. OCM substrate, enzyme, and methylation terms were combined with oocyte, follicular, ovarian, embryo, and ART terms. Reference lists were hand-searched. Evidence was assessed by species, tissue, design, and outcome, prioritizing mechanistic perturbations, prospective cohorts, and clinical trials.

OUTCOMES: Mouse studies indicate transient leucovorin uptake in growing oocytes and cumulus-supported serine transfer. Methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) phosphorylation shows developmental regulation, whereas methionine adenosyltransferase 2A (MAT2A) inhibition after the eight-cell stage reduces bovine blastocyst development. DNA methylation supports imprint establishment; reducing methionine from 50 to 10 µM in bovine embryo culture altered methylation at more than 1600 genes. Porcine homocysteine (Hcy) exposure impaired mitochondrial and developmental endpoints, with an increased mitochondrial DNA 16S methylation signal whose causal role requires validation. Multi-component supplementation containing 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF) increased bovine blastocyst yield during maturation (34.9% vs 17.1%), without establishing a human protocol. In a selected recurrent-miscarriage/repeated-ART-failure cohort, over 40% were homozygous or compound heterozygous for common MTHFR variants, without appropriate controls establishing enrichment. Higher Hcy was associated with poorer implantation and clinical pregnancy in C677T TT carriers; a separate retrospective cohort associated hyperhomocysteinaemia with lower live birth (adjusted odds ratio [OR] approximately 0.45). Another retrospective study associated combined 5-MTHF/B12/B6 supplementation with higher clinical pregnancy (OR 2.03) and live birth (OR 1.83) than folic acid alone, without establishing causality. One study found optimal red-cell folate in 12% and adequate B12 in 44% of infertile women. Age-associated methylation remodelling varies across loci, cells, and models; blood/somatic methylation-age estimates are not oocyte-age measurements. Experimental reproductive studies suggest context-dependent H2S roles, but effects on chronological ovarian ageing and oocyte demethylation remain unestablished. Non-reproductive experiments on H2S-linked MTHFR persulfidation support further study of sulfur-dependent Hcy partitioning, without validating this mechanism in oocytes.

WIDER IMPLICATIONS: OCM provides a rationale for investigating nutritional interventions, but predominantly animal and observational evidence does not establish improved human live birth or delayed ovarian ageing. Priorities include direct OCM flux and measurements of SAM and S-adenosylhomocysteine in ageing human oocytes, adequately powered randomized trials assessing reproductive outcomes and safety, and tissue-specific methylation measures as exploratory biomarkers. Common MTHFR variants alone should not determine treatment.

REGISTRATION NUMBER: N/A.
